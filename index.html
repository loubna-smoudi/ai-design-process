Research & Discovery

Get up to speed on any project in hours instead of days. Synthesize interviews, documents, and data into structured insights you can design from directly.

Microsoft Copilot ChatPwC (Opus) Copilot Agent
Transcribe interviews and meetings automatically
Lisette · Holly · Sydney · Mi · Shikha · Loubna · Robyn
Steps to follow
1
Before the session starts, enable Copilot in your Microsoft Teams meeting — click the Copilot icon in the meeting toolbar.
2
Run the session as normal. Copilot generates a live transcript in the background automatically.
3
After the session, download the transcript from Microsoft Stream — Teams → Meetings → Recordings & Transcripts.
4
Keep the video recording on during the session — you'll use it later to QA the AI synthesis output against what was actually said.
Always QA the output. Transcripts drop words and mangle names. Cross-check every AI synthesis against your own notes or the recording before using it in a design decision.
Copilot conversations are subject to PwC data retention policies and may be deleted. Export and save anything important — don't treat Copilot as a permanent record.
Synthesize transcripts into structured design insights
Sydney · Shikha · Mi · Lisette · Holly · Robyn
Steps to follow
1
Gather all project materials: SOW, discovery transcripts, prior research decks, meeting notes.
2
Attach an interview guide template as a reference artifact so the AI understands your research structure.
3
Define your synthesis framework first — decide what you want out: key themes + quotes, problem statements, opportunity areas, etc. Don't let the AI choose the structure for you.
4
Attach all files to ChatPwC (Opus) and run the prompt below.
5
Download as Word. Open and refine manually — fill in nuance the AI missed.
6
Ask follow-up probing questions: "What quotes support that theme?" or "Which participants mentioned X most?"
Synthesis prompt — paste into ChatPwC with files attached
I'm a UX designer on [Project Name]. Attached are [N] interview transcripts from sessions with [stakeholder type].

Synthesize these into a structured research output:

1. Top 5–7 themes — each with a one-line summary and 2–3 direct quotes
2. Core user pain points — ranked by how frequently they came up
3. Design implications — for each theme, what should the design do differently?
4. Contradictions — anything participants disagreed on that I should probe further
5. Open questions — things that came up but weren't resolved

Be specific. Cite which participant role said what. Format for Word.
AI flattens nuance. Never use synthesis output directly as a design brief — it's a starting point that needs your editorial judgment.
Get up to speed on a new project from existing documentation

Project onboarding prompt
I'm a UX designer just joining [Project Name]. Based on all the documentation available, answer these:

1. What problem are we solving and for whom?
2. What has already been built or designed?
3. What UX improvements or future state have been discussed?
4. Who are the key stakeholders and what does each care about most?
5. What are the current open questions or unresolved decisions?
6. What should I read first to get up to speed fastest?

Cite the specific meeting, document, or deck behind each answer. If something is uncertain, say so.
Shikha: "Joined a project with years of meeting history. Ran Copilot through all transcripts and arrived at my first meeting already knowing what they'd been discussing for months."
Build a Copilot onboarding agent for the project
Loubna
Steps to follow
1
Open Copilot Studio and create a new agent.
2
Under "Source files," add all onboarding materials: transcripts, Word docs, Excel sheets, PPTs, SharePoint pages.
3
Restrict the agent to source files only. This is critical — without this restriction, the agent will hallucinate project-specific details from general knowledge.
4
Paste the system instructions below as the agent's behaviour prompt.
5
Run the starter prompts to verify the agent is working correctly before sharing with the team.
Full agent system instructions — paste into Copilot Studio
You are the onboarding assistant for [Project Name]. Help new team members become productive quickly using only the connected project materials.

Use ONLY the connected files as your knowledge source. Do not draw from general knowledge.

When answering:
- Lead with a clear answer, then provide supporting detail
- Cite the document or transcript behind each claim, including its date
- Distinguish between: Approved decisions / Proposed ideas / Assumptions / Open questions
- If sources conflict, explain the conflict — do not choose silently
- Never invent information — say what isn't covered and suggest who to ask

When summarizing meetings, always separate:
- Decisions made
- Requirements confirmed
- Action items and owners
- Unresolved questions
- Background context only

For project overviews, always cover:
- Purpose and expected business outcomes
- Scope and what's out of scope
- Key stakeholders, their roles, and priorities
- Timeline, milestones, and current status
- Risks, dependencies, and open questions
- Recommended reading order for a new team member
Starter prompts to verify the agent is working
Give me an executive briefing — what are we building, why, and for whom?

Who are all the key stakeholders and team members, and what is each person's role?

What are the main business and technical requirements?

Are there any contradicting requirements or outdated decisions I should know about?

What are the key user pain points we're solving for?
Understand complex data before designing for it
Shikha · Mi · Robyn
Steps to follow
1
Open the Excel file you'll be designing for.
2
Open Copilot directly inside Excel via the Copilot icon in the ribbon — don't copy data into a chat window.
3
Start broad — ask the big questions first before getting specific. Use the prompts below.
4
Use the answers to make informed design decisions about how to visualize and structure data in Figma before opening it.
Data exploration prompts — run inside Excel with Copilot
What is this dataset tracking? Give me a plain-language summary of what it contains and what it measures.

What are the most important patterns, trends, or outliers in this data?

If I had to design a dashboard to surface the most critical information, what should be front and centre?

Define your research synthesis framework before you start
RecommendedRun before every research phase
Without a defined structure, AI synthesis outputs are inconsistent across projects and designers. Running this step before research starts means every output looks the same, is easier to compare, and feeds directly into design work.
Steps to follow
1
Before starting research, paste the prompt below into ChatPwC with the project brief attached.
2
Review the output framework and adjust to fit your project's specific questions and stakeholder needs.
3
Share with the team — everyone uses the same structure for synthesis on that project. Paste it as context into every subsequent synthesis session.
Framework design prompt
I'm about to start a UX research phase for [Project Name]. Based on the attached project brief, help me define a research synthesis framework.

The framework should tell me:
1. What are the 4–6 most important questions this research needs to answer?
2. What synthesis structure should I use? (e.g. themes + quotes, jobs-to-be-done, pain/gain, etc.)
3. What does "good synthesis" look like for this project — what outputs do stakeholders need?
4. What are the risks of bias or gaps I should watch for?

Output a one-page framework document I can share with the team before research begins.

What's working

  • Hours of reading compressed into minutes
  • Arriving at first meetings already fully informed
  • Onboarding agent removes dependency on any one person
  • Data analysis reveals patterns not visible by eye

Tensions & concerns

  • AI misses nuance — QA against recordings is non-negotiable
  • AI sometimes invents or fills gaps in requirements incorrectly
  • Copilot conversations deleted by retention policies
  • Repetitive output across similar documents

Next steps

  • Standardize synthesis prompts so all outputs look the same
  • Build shared prompt library — Sydney's prompts as starting templates
  • Define a QA checklist for every AI synthesis output

Brainstorming & Ideation

Move from research insights to divergent design directions fast. No blank canvas. No generic outputs. Use AI to generate, constrain, and evaluate concepts before committing to a direction.

ChatPwC (Opus) Figma Make Copilot
Frame design directions from research insights
Shikha · Sydney
Steps to follow
1
Complete Phase 1 synthesis first. You need concrete insights before this step — don't skip it.
2
Paste your synthesized insights into ChatPwC as the starting context.
3
Run the prompt below. You want conceptual directions — not wireframes, not visual styles. Thinking before pixels.
4
Use the output as a conceptual foundation before you open Figma — so you're exploring directions, not starting from nothing.
Direction-framing prompt
I've just completed user research for [Project Name]. Here are the synthesized insights:
[Paste your research synthesis here]

Based on these, propose 3–5 distinct design directions. For each:
- Name: a memorable 2–3 word title
- Premise: one sentence describing the core idea
- Which user pain points it directly addresses
- The key interaction or experience principle behind it
- What would be true about this design that wouldn't be true about the others
- Main risk or assumption to validate

Make these genuinely different from each other — not variations on one theme.
Craft a constraint-aware prompt for Figma Make
Shikha · Sydney · Loubna
An unconstrained prompt produces visually interesting but technically unbuildable outputs. Baking platform limits into the prompt before running it means every output is something you can actually ship.
Steps to follow
1
Define your platform constraints — what components and interactions are actually available in the target environment?
2
Open ChatPwC and run the prompt-writing prompt below — give it your design direction, research context, and constraints.
3
Review and refine the output before running it in Figma Make.
4
Add this line to the final prompt: "Use as few credits as possible. Ask me clarifying questions before executing."
Prompt to write your Figma Make prompt
Help me write a Figma Make prompt for a design I need to create.

Context:
- Project: [Project Name]
- What we're designing: [describe the screen or feature]
- Target platform: [e.g. SharePoint / iOS app / internal web tool]
- Design direction: [paste the chosen direction]
- Key user needs: [2–3 bullet points from research]

Constraints (design MUST respect these):
- [Platform-specific limits, e.g. "SharePoint web parts only — no custom CSS"]
- [Design system or component library constraints]
- [Screen size or device requirements]

Write a Figma Make prompt that is specific enough to produce something useful, but open enough to allow creative variation. Keep the language direct and free of jargon.
Shikha's credit-saving technique: Tell both ChatPwC and Figma Make to "use as few credits as possible and ask clarifying questions before executing anything." Keeps you in control, prevents wasted runs.
Generate multiple design directions with Figma Make
Holly · Noah · Mi · Shikha · Loubna
Steps to follow

2

Evaluate and score design directions before stakeholder review
RecommendedBefore every stakeholder review
Going into a review with 3 equally valid directions puts the decision in the stakeholder's hands. This step gives you a structured rationale for your recommendation before the room decides by gut feel.
Steps to follow
1
Paste a summary of each design direction into ChatPwC with your research insights and project goals.
2
Run the prompt below to get a structured evaluation.
3
Use the output to form your own recommendation — don't let the AI make the call. Use it to structure your thinking and build your rationale.
Direction evaluation prompt
I have [N] design directions for [Project Name]. Evaluate them against what matters most.

Project goals: [paste from brief or PRD]
User needs from research: [paste top 3–4 findings]
Technical constraints: [list platform/system limits]

Directions:
Direction 1: [name + one paragraph description]
Direction 2: [name + one paragraph description]
Direction 3: [name + one paragraph description]

Score each (1–5) on:
- User need fit: how directly it addresses the core user pain points
- Technical feasibility: how buildable within the constraints
- Business alignment: how well it supports project goals
- Design risk: how much uncertainty it relies on (lower = better)

Then give me a clear recommendation with a 2–3 sentence rationale for stakeholder presentation.
Generate realistic mock data without exposing client information
Shikha · Loubna

Steps to follow

Use the real Excel file as a structural reference only — look at column headers, data types, and format. Do not copy real values.
2
Open Copilot and describe the structure. Run the prompt below.

Download the synthetic dataset as Excel or CSV.

Use this dataset in Figma Make — realistic-looking designs with zero data risk.
Mock data generation prompt
I'm a UX designer who needs realistic-looking fake data for design mockups. I cannot use real client data.

Data structure I'm designing for:
[Describe columns and types, e.g. "Employee Name (text), Department (Finance/HR/IT/Operations), Status (Active/Inactive), Date Joined (DD/MM/YYYY), Budget ($CAD)"]

Generate 25 rows of realistic fake data that:
- Matches this exact structure and format
- Uses plausible-sounding fake names, values, and dates
- Includes a realistic distribution (not all the same status or department)
- Includes 2–3 edge cases to stress-test the design (very long name, missing value, maximum number)

Output as a table I can paste directly into Excel.

What's working

  • Rapid variations without blank canvas paralysis
  • Research insights feed directly into design framing
  • Constraint-based prompts cut unbuildable outputs significantly
  • Structured evaluation makes stakeholder reviews sharper

Tensions & concerns

  • AI directions feel generic — heavy curation still required
  • Different prompt styles across the team produce inconsistent outputs
  • Easy to burn tokens on variations nobody ends up using

Next steps

  • Define a standard PRD → low-fi prompt for the whole team
  • Standardize mock data prompt using Copilot + Excel
  • Make direction evaluation a required step before every stakeholder review

Cross-check your design against the original requirements
RecommendedBefore every stakeholder design review
It's easy to drift from original requirements when iterating with AI. This step catches gaps before stakeholders do — so you're not walking into a review with a design that misses a must-have feature.
Steps to follow
1
Export your current design as screenshots or a PDF walkthrough.
2
Attach it to ChatPwC along with the original PRD or requirements document.
3
Run the prompt below to get a requirements traceability check.
4
Address any gaps identified before the review. Don't let the stakeholder be the one to find them.
Requirements traceability prompt
I've attached two things:
1. The project requirements document (PRD)
2. Screenshots of my current design

Review the design against the requirements and tell me:

1. Which requirements are clearly addressed — cite the screen or element for each
2. Which are partially addressed — what's missing or unclear
3. Which appear to be missing entirely from the design
4. Any functionality in the design that doesn't trace back to a stated requirement (scope creep risk)

Format as a table: Requirement | Status (Addressed / Partial / Missing) | Evidence or Gap

Run the file hygiene Figma skill — clean layers, spacing & naming
Loubna · Noah
One-time setup
1
In Figma, open the Agents panel (bottom left). Click "+" → Skills → Add New Skill.
2
Click "Start from scratch," name it "File Hygiene," and paste the instructions below.
File hygiene skill instructions
Perform a complete visual-consistency and file-hygiene pass on this Figma file.

Objectives:
- Make spacing, padding, alignment, and visual rhythm consistent
- Clean up layer structure and naming
- Remove genuinely unused content without touching components, variants, or prototypes
- Preserve the existing visual design, brand, content, and functionality

Before editing:
- Create a backup named "Before cleanup – [date]"
- Audit existing spacing patterns, variables, styles, and components
- Infer the dominant design system — do not introduce a new one
- If no spacing system exists, use a 4px base grid (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48px)

Layer cleanup rules:
- Rename frames, groups, and components with clear, descriptive names
- Remove empty groups, empty frames, and zero-size layers
- Remove hidden layers ONLY after verifying they aren't used by: component variants, prototype interactions, overlays, masks, or exports
- Move anything uncertain to a "Cleanup – Review" page instead of deleting

Guardrails:
- Do not rewrite content or change the information hierarchy
- Do not break prototype links, interactions, or component properties
- Do not make subjective redesign changes

End with a cleanup report: pages modified / spacing system used / layers deleted / items moved to review / unresolved inconsistencies.
How to run it
1
Select all the mockups you want to clean.
2
Agents panel → "Use Skill" → "File Hygiene."
3
Review the cleanup report and check the "Cleanup – Review" page before confirming any deletions.
Supervise this skill — it can occasionally remove intentional hidden layers. Always review the "Cleanup – Review" page before confirming deletions.

Combined bug + accessibility audit prompt
Please run two audits and fix what you find:

1. Bug audit — identify and fix:
   - JavaScript errors or broken interactions
   - Visual inconsistencies between design and implementation
   - Broken navigation flows or unexpected behavior
   - Console errors or warnings

2. Accessibility audit (WCAG 2.1 AA) — identify and fix:
   - Missing or incorrect ARIA labels and roles
   - Color contrast below 4.5:1 (normal text) or 3:1 (large text)
   - Keyboard navigation issues — focus order, missing focus indicators
   - Missing alt text on images and icons
   - Form inputs without associated labels
   - Interactive elements unreachable by keyboard

After fixing, summarize: what you found, what you fixed, and anything that needs a design decision before it can be resolved.

Based on this prototype codebase, generate a developer handoff specification document.

Include:

Feedback extraction prompt
I just presented design work to stakeholders. Based on this meeting transcript, give me:

1. A prioritized list of changes requested — most critical first, with a brief reason for each
2. Screen-specific feedback — for each screen or feature mentioned, what was the feedback?
3. Open questions to resolve before making changes
4. Decisions made in the meeting I should document

Write a design decision log before you close the project
RecommendedAt project close
Six months after launch, nobody remembers why a decision was made. A decision log means future designers understand the reasoning behind every significant choice, and don't undo good work by accident.
Steps to follow
1
Export your Figma file as screenshots and attach to ChatPwC along with the PRD and research synthesis.
2
Run the prompt below. The AI drafts the structure and options considered — you fill in the rationale column with context only you know.
3
Include in the handoff package alongside the Figma file, GitHub repo, and dev spec.
Design decision log prompt
I'm closing out a design project and need to document key design decisions. Based on the attached PRD, research synthesis, and design screenshots, build a design decision log.

For each significant design decision you can identify:
- Decision: what was decided (e.g. "Use a step-by-step wizard instead of a single form")
- Alternatives considered: what other approaches were on the table
- Rationale: [leave as TBD — I will fill this in]
- Trade-offs: what we gained and what we gave up
- Assumptions: what has to be true for this decision to remain valid
- Revisit trigger: what would cause us to reconsider this decision

Focus on decisions that are non-obvious or where a future designer might be tempted to change them without understanding why. Format as a table.

Five principles the team works by
01
AI compresses repetition, not judgment

Use AI for the slow, mechanical parts. Which direction to pursue, what the user needs, what makes the experience right — that stays with you.

02
Each tool feeds into the next

Copilot → ChatPwC → Figma Make → Claude Code. The output of one step is the input to the next. Build the chain deliberately from the start of every project.

03
Always QA the output

Every AI output needs a human review pass before it's used. Synthesized research, generated designs, brand checks, dev specs — confident-sounding hallucinations are real.

04
Constrain prompts to reality

Bake platform and design system limits into prompts before running them — not after. Unconstrained prompts produce unbuildable work that wastes everyone's time.

05
Standardize to scale

Strong individual workflows need to become shared prompt libraries. Knowledge siloed in one person's head doesn't count as team capability.
