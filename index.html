Research & Discovery

Get up to speed on any project in hours instead of days. Synthesize interviews, docs, and data into structured insights you can design from directly.

Microsoft Copilot ChatPwC (Opus) Copilot Agent
🎙️
Transcribe interviews and meetings automatically
Lisette · Holly · Sydney · Mi · Shikha · Loubna · Robyn
▼
How to do it
  1. 1Before the session, enable Copilot in your Microsoft Teams meeting — click the Copilot icon in the meeting toolbar.
  2. 2Run the session as normal. Copilot generates a live transcript in the background.
  3. 3After the session, download the transcript from Microsoft Stream: Teams → Meetings → Recordings & Transcripts.
  4. 4Keep the video recording on during the session — use it to QA AI synthesis outputs against what was actually said.
⚠Always QA: Transcripts can be unreliable — words get dropped, names get mangled. Cross-check every AI synthesis output against your own session notes or the recording before using it in a design decision.
💡Copilot conversations are subject to PwC data retention policies and may be deleted. Don't treat them as your permanent project record — export and save anything important.

Synthesize transcripts into structured design insights
Sydney · Shikha · Mi · Lisette · Holly · Robyn
▼
Sydney's workflow — step by step
  1. 1Gather all project materials: SOW, stakeholder discovery transcripts, prior research decks, meeting notes.
  2. 2Attach an interview guide template as a reference artifact so the AI knows the research structure.
  3. 3Define your synthesis framework before prompting — decide what you want out: key themes + quotes, problem statements, opportunity areas, etc.
  4. 4Attach all files to ChatPwC (Opus) and run the prompt below.
  5. 5Download as Word. Open and refine — fill in nuance the AI missed.
  6. 6Ask follow-up probing questions: "What quotes support that theme?" or "Which participants mentioned X most?"
Synthesis prompt — paste into ChatPwC with files attached
I'm a UX designer on [Project Name]. Attached are [N] interview transcripts from sessions with [stakeholder type].

Synthesize these into a structured research output with:

1. Top 5–7 themes — each with a one-line summary and 2–3 direct quotes from participants
2. Core user pain points — ranked by how frequently they came up
3. Design implications — for each theme, what should the design do differently?
4. Contradictions or tensions — anything participants disagreed on that I should probe further
5. Open questions — things that came up but weren't resolved

Format as a clean document I can paste into Word. Be specific — cite which participant (by role, not name) said what.
⚠AI can over-simplify or flatten nuance from real sessions. Never use synthesis output directly as a design brief — it's a starting point that requires your own editorial judgment.

Get up to speed on a new project from existing documentation
Holly · Shikha · Loubna · Robyn
▼
How to do it
  1. 1Don't wait for a catch-up meeting. Open Copilot with WorkIQ and Opus turned on.
  2. 2Point it at the project's SharePoint folder or upload the documents directly.
  3. 3Run the starter prompt below. Ask it to cite every document it pulls from.
  4. 4Follow up with more specific questions as gaps emerge.
Project onboarding prompt
I'm a UX designer just joining [Project Name]. Based on all the documentation available, answer these:

1. What problem are we solving and for whom?
2. What has already been built or designed — what exists today?
3. What UX improvements or future state have been discussed?
4. Who are the key stakeholders, and what does each one care about most?
5. What are the current open questions or unresolved decisions?
6. What should I read first to get up to speed fastest?

Cite the specific meeting, document, or deck that supports each answer. If something is uncertain, say so.
💡Shikha: "Joined a project with a long meeting history. Ran Copilot through all previous transcripts and arrived at my first meeting already knowing what they'd been discussing for months."

Build a Copilot onboarding agent for the project
Loubna
▼
One-time setup
  1. 1Open Copilot Studio and create a new agent.
  2. 2In "Source files," add all onboarding materials: transcripts, Word docs, Excel sheets, PPTs, SharePoint pages.
  3. 3Restrict the agent to only use source files. This prevents hallucination from general knowledge.
  4. 4Paste the system instructions below as the agent's behavior prompt.
Full agent system instructions
You are the onboarding assistant for [Project Name]. Your purpose is to help new project members become productive quickly using the connected project materials.

Use ONLY the connected SharePoint files, transcripts, presentations, requirements documents, and project records as your knowledge sources. Do not draw from general knowledge.

When answering:
- Lead with a clear, concise answer — then provide supporting detail
- Cite the document, deck, or transcript supporting each claim, including its date
- Distinguish clearly between: Approved decisions / Proposed ideas / Assumptions / Open questions
- If sources conflict, explain the conflict — do not silently choose one
- Never invent missing information. Say "this isn't covered in the available materials" and suggest who to ask
- Explain project-specific terms, acronyms, and systems in plain language

When summarizing meetings, always separate:
- Decisions made
- Requirements confirmed
- Action items and owners
- Unresolved questions
- Background context

For project overviews, always cover:
- Project purpose and expected business outcomes
- Current scope and what's out of scope
- Main business and technical requirements
- Key stakeholders, their roles, and what they care about
- Timeline, milestones, and current status
- Risks, dependencies, and unresolved questions
- Recommended reading order for a new team member
Starter prompts to run once the agent is live
Give me an executive briefing on this project — what are we building, why, and for whom?

Who are all the key stakeholders and team members? What is each person's role?

What are the main business and technical requirements?

Are there any contradicting requirements or outdated decisions I should know about?

What are the key user pain points we're designing to solve?
⚠If the agent is not restricted to source files, it will fabricate project-specific details from general knowledge. Always enable the source-file restriction before sharing with the team.

Define your research synthesis framework before you start
NewRecommended before every research phase
▼
Why this matters
  1. !Without a defined structure, AI synthesis outputs are inconsistent across projects and designers. Running this step before interviews means every research output looks the same, is easier to compare, and feeds directly into design work.
How to do it
  1. 1Before starting research, paste the prompt below into ChatPwC with your project brief attached.
  2. 2Review the output framework and adjust to fit your project's specific questions.
  3. 3Save the agreed framework and share with the team — everyone uses the same structure for synthesis on that project.
Framework design prompt
I'm about to start a UX research phase for [Project Name]. Based on the attached project brief, help me define a research synthesis framework.

The framework should tell me:
1. What are the 4–6 most important questions this research needs to answer?
2. What synthesis structure should I use to capture findings? (e.g. themes + quotes, jobs-to-be-done, pain/gain map, etc.)
3. What does "good synthesis" look like for this specific project — what outputs do stakeholders and the design team need?
4. What are the risks of bias or gaps I should watch for given this research context?

Output a one-page framework document I can share with the team before research begins.

  2. 2Upload the screenshots into ChatPwC.
  3. 3Run the prompt below to extract all labels, content, and structure as editable text.

Content extraction prompt
I've attached screenshots of an existing product design. Please extract all of the following:

- All text labels, headings, and body copy (exactly as written)
- All navigation items and menu labels
- All button labels and CTAs
- The page/screen structure and information hierarchy
- All form field labels and input types
- Any table or data column headers
- Any status labels, tags, or system messages

Organise by screen. I'll use this as the content foundation for a redesign.

What's working

  • Hours of reading compressed into minutes
  • Arriving at first meetings already fully informed
  • Onboarding agent removes dependency on any one person for context
  • Data analysis reveals patterns not visible by eye

Tensions & concerns

  • AI misses nuance from real sessions — QA is non-negotiable
  • AI sometimes invents or fills gaps in requirements incorrectly
  • Copilot conversations deleted by retention policies — don't rely on them as records
  • Repetitive output when synthesizing multiple similar documents

Next steps

  • Standardize synthesis prompts across the team so all outputs look the same
  • Build shared prompt library — Sydney's prompts as starting templates
  • Define a QA checklist for every AI synthesis output

Brainstorming & Ideation

Move from research insights to divergent design directions without starting from a blank canvas. Use AI to generate, constrain, and evaluate concepts before committing to a direction.

ChatPwC (Opus) Figma Make Copilot
💡
Frame design directions from research insights
Shikha · Sydney
▼
How to do it
  1. 1Complete Phase 1 synthesis first. You need concrete insights before this step — don't skip it.
  2. 2Paste your synthesized insights into ChatPwC as the starting context.
  3. 3Run the prompt below. The goal is to get conceptual directions — not wireframes, not visual styles. Thinking, not pixels.
  4. 4This becomes the conceptual foundation before you open Figma.
Direction-framing prompt
I've just completed user research for [Project Name]. Here are the synthesized insights:

[Paste your research synthesis here]

Based on these insights, propose 3–5 distinct design directions I could explore. For each:
- Name: a memorable 2–3 word title
- Premise: one sentence describing the core idea
- Which user pain points or needs it directly addresses
- The key interaction or experience principle behind it
- What would be true about this design that wouldn't be true about the others
- Main risk or assumption to validate

Make these genuinely different from each other — not variations on one idea. I want real divergence.
💡Sydney: "Feed the PRD into ChatPwC and ask it to produce a low-fi concept or interface structure based on its understanding — use that as the starting point for your Figma Make prompt."
✍️
Craft a constraint-aware prompt for Figma Make
Shikha · Sydney · Loubna
▼
Why constraints matter
  1. !An unconstrained prompt produces visually interesting but technically unbuildable outputs. Baking platform and design system limits into the prompt before running it means every output is something you can actually ship.
How to do it
  1. 1Define your platform constraints: what components, patterns, and interactions are actually available in the target environment?
  2. 2Open ChatPwC and run the prompt below to draft a well-structured Figma Make prompt.
  3. 3Review and refine the output before running it in Figma Make.
  4. 4Add this instruction to the final prompt: "Use as few credits as possible. Ask me clarifying questions before executing."
Prompt to write your Figma Make prompt
Help me write a Figma Make prompt for a design I need to create.

Context:
- Project: [Project Name]
- What we're designing: [describe the screen or feature]
- Target platform / environment: [e.g. SharePoint / iOS app / internal web tool]
- Design direction: [paste the chosen direction from the previous step]
- Key user needs it must address: [2–3 bullet points from research]

Platform constraints (the design MUST respect these):
- [List what components or interactions are available, e.g. "SharePoint web parts only — no custom CSS"]
- [Any design system or token constraints]
- [Any screen size or device requirements]

Write a Figma Make prompt that produces a strong first design attempt — specific enough to be useful, open enough to allow variation. Keep the language direct and free of jargon.
💡Shikha's credit-saving technique: tell Figma Make to "use as few credits as possible and ask clarifying questions before executing anything." Stays in control, prevents wasted runs.

Generate multiple design directions with Figma Make
Holly · Noah · Mi · Shikha · Loubna
▼
How to do it
  1. 1Open Figma Make and paste the constraint-aware prompt from Step 2.2.
  2. 2Ask explicitly for multiple variations: "Generate 10 different versions of this."
  3. 3Review outputs — the goal is to think differently and break out of your defaults, not to ship any of them directly.
  4. 4Pick the 2–3 most interesting directions and bring them to stakeholders for early feedback.
Noah's approach — force divergent thinking
  1. 1Describe your initial idea in plain language to Figma Make.
  2. 2Explicitly ask for 10 different versions. Don't settle for 3.
  3. 3Use the outputs to challenge your own assumptions — not to find an answer but to see what you haven't considered.
Mi's approach — use existing design as input
  1. 1Upload your current design into Figma Make.
  2. 2Prompt: "Here is my current design. Generate 5 different layout and visual approaches that keep the same core function but explore completely different ways to present it."
  3. 3Compare and rethink the flow and layout based on what emerges.
⚠AI-generated directions often feel generic. They're a prompt for your own thinking — not a finished concept. Heavy designer curation is always required.
⚖️
Evaluate and score design directions before stakeholder review
NewRecommended before every stakeholder review
▼
Why this step matters
  1. !Going into a stakeholder review with 3 directions that feel equally valid puts the design decision in the stakeholder's hands. This step gives you a structured rationale for which direction you're recommending — and why — before the room decides by gut feel.
How to do it
  1. 1After generating directions, paste the summary of each into ChatPwC with your research insights and project goals.
  2. 2Run the evaluation prompt below.
  3. 3Use the output to form your own recommendation — don't let the AI make the call, use it to structure your thinking.
  4. 4Present your recommended direction at the stakeholder review with clear rationale behind it.
Direction evaluation prompt
I have [N] design directions for [Project Name]. Help me evaluate them against what matters most.

Project goals: [paste goals from brief or PRD]
User needs from research: [paste top 3–4 findings]
Technical constraints: [list platform/system limits]

Here are the design directions:
Direction 1: [name + one paragraph description]
Direction 2: [name + one paragraph description]
Direction 3: [name + one paragraph description]

For each direction, score it (1–5) on:
- User need fit: how directly it addresses the core user pain points
- Technical feasibility: how buildable it is within the constraints
- Business alignment: how well it supports the stated project goals
- Design risk: how much uncertainty or assumption it relies on (lower is better)

Then give me a clear recommendation with a 2–3 sentence rationale I can use in a stakeholder presentation.

Generate realistic mock data without exposing client information
Shikha · Loubna
▼
🔒Security first: Never put actual client data into Figma Make. Copilot is more secure for data handling. Generate synthetic data in Copilot, then use it in Figma.
How to do it
  1. 1Use the real Excel file as a structural reference only — look at column headers, data types, and format.
  2. 2Open Copilot and run the prompt below — describe the structure, not the actual data.
  3. 3Download the synthetic dataset as Excel or CSV.
  4. 4Use this dataset in Figma Make — realistic-looking designs with zero data risk.
Mock data generation prompt
I'm a UX designer who needs realistic-looking fake data for design mockups. I cannot use real client data.

Here is the data structure I'm designing for:
[Describe columns and types, e.g. "Employee Name (text), Department (Finance/HR/IT/Operations), Status (Active/Inactive), Date Joined (DD/MM/YYYY), Budget ($CAD)"]

Generate 25 rows of realistic fake data that:
- Matches this exact structure and format
- Uses plausible-sounding fake names, values, and dates
- Includes a realistic distribution (not all the same status or department)
- Includes 2–3 edge cases to stress-test the design (e.g. very long name, missing value, maximum number)

Output as a table I can paste into Excel.

What's working

  • Rapid concept variations without blank canvas paralysis
  • Research insights feed directly into design framing
  • Constraint-based prompts cut unbuildable outputs significantly
  • Structured direction evaluation makes stakeholder reviews sharper

Tensions & concerns

  • AI directions feel generic — heavy curation still required
  • Different prompt styles across the team produce inconsistent outputs
  • Easy to spend tokens on variations nobody needs

Next steps

  • Define a standard PRD → low-fi prompt for the whole team
  • Standardize mock data prompt using Copilot + Excel
  • Make direction evaluation a required step before every stakeholder review

NewRecommended before any stakeholder design review

Requirements traceability prompt
I've attached two things:
1. The project requirements document (PRD)
2. Screenshots of my current design

Please review the design against the requirements and tell me:

1. Which requirements are clearly addressed in the design — cite the screen or element that covers each one
2. Which requirements are partially addressed — what's missing or unclear
3. Which requirements appear to be missing entirely from the design
4. Any functionality in the design that doesn't trace back to a stated requirement (scope creep risk)

Format as a table: Requirement | Status (Addressed / Partial / Missing) | Evidence or Gap

QA, Annotation & Handoff

Deliver clean, accessible, brand-compliant files ready for development — with minimal manual cleanup and a clear record of every decision.

Figma Skills Claude Code Copilot ChatPwC GitHub
🧹
Run the file hygiene Figma skill — clean layers, spacing & naming
Loubna · Noah
▼
One-time setup
  1. 1In Figma, open the Agents panel (bottom left). Click "+" → Skills → Add New Skill.
  2. 2Click "Start from scratch," name it "File Hygiene," and paste the instructions below.
File hygiene skill instructions
Perform a complete visual-consistency and file-hygiene pass on this Figma file.

Primary objectives:
- Make spacing, padding, alignment, sizing, and visual rhythm consistent
- Clean up layer structure and naming
- Remove genuinely unused content without touching components, variants, prototypes, or responsive behavior
- Preserve the existing visual design, brand, content, and functionality

Before editing:
- Create a backup named "Before cleanup – [date]"
- Audit existing spacing patterns, variables, styles, components, and grids
- Infer the dominant design system in use — do not introduce a new one
- If no spacing system exists, use a 4px base grid (common values: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48px)

Layout and spacing:
- Standardize padding and gaps across components serving the same purpose
- Align objects to shared edges, centres, columns, and baselines
- Correct accidental 1–2px misalignments
- Use Auto Layout where it improves consistency
- Ensure repeated screens use consistent margins, content widths, and section spacing

Layer cleanup:
- Rename frames, groups, and components with clear, descriptive names
- Remove empty groups, empty frames, zero-size layers, and abandoned duplicates
- Remove hidden layers ONLY after verifying they are not used by: component variants, prototype interactions, overlays, masks, or exports
- Move anything uncertain to a "Cleanup – Review" page instead of deleting

Guardrails:
- Do not rewrite content or change the information hierarchy
- Do not break prototype links, interactions, component properties, or exports
- Do not make subjective redesign changes

When finished, provide a cleanup report: pages modified / spacing system used / layers deleted / items moved to review / unresolved inconsistencies.
How to run it
  1. 1Select all the mockups you want to clean.
  2. 2In the Agents panel, click "Use Skill" → "File Hygiene."
  3. 3Review the cleanup report and check the "Cleanup – Review" page for anything flagged as uncertain before confirming.
⚠Supervise this skill — it can occasionally remove intentional hidden layers. Always review the "Cleanup – Review" page before confirming any deletions.

NewRecommended at project close
▼
Why this matters
  1. !Six months after launch, nobody remembers why a decision was made. A design decision log means future designers — or the same team in a later phase — understand the reasoning behind every significant design choice, and don't undo good decisions by accident.
How to do it
  1. 1Export your Figma file as screenshots and attach to ChatPwC along with the project PRD and your research synthesis.
  2. 2Run the prompt below at the end of the project.

Design decision log prompt
I'm at the end of a design project and need to document the key design decisions made. Based on the attached PRD, research synthesis, and design screenshots, help me build a design decision log.

For each significant design decision you can identify, provide:
- Decision: what was decided (e.g. "Use a step-by-step wizard instead of a single form")
- Alternatives considered: what other approaches were on the table
- Rationale: [I will fill this in — leave as TBD for now]
- Trade-offs: what we gained and what we gave up
- Assumptions: what has to be true for this decision to remain valid
- Revisit trigger: what would cause us to reconsider this decision

Focus on decisions that are non-obvious or where a future designer might be tempted to change them without understanding why they were made.

Five principles the team works by
01
AI compresses repetition, not judgment

Use AI for the slow, mechanical parts. Which direction to pursue, what the user actually needs, what makes the experience right — that's yours.

02
Each tool feeds into the next

Copilot → ChatPwC → Figma Make → Claude Code. The output of one step is the input to the next. Build the chain deliberately.

03
Always QA the output

Synthesized research, generated designs, brand checks, dev specs — every AI output needs a human review pass before it's used. Confident-sounding hallucinations are real.

04
Constrain prompts to reality

Bake platform and design system limits into prompts before running them — not after. Unconstrained prompts produce unbuildable work.

05
Standardize to scale

Strong individual workflows need to become shared prompt libraries. Knowledge siloed in one person's brain doesn't count as team capability.
